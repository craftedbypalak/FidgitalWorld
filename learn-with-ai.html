The Idea

AI can hand kids the answer. Or it can hand them the "aha."

A tutor that learns how they learn: song, video, story, or podcast, whatever clicks. That's the AI we teach kids to use.

The Journey

Six "aha" stops. Two sessions.
However it fits your week.

Session 1: Think & Learn (about 2 hrs)

💬Talking to AI

The one skill that makes every other stop easier.

  • Ask clear, specific questions instead of vague ones
  • Give AI an example to work from
  • Ask it to try again, a different way, when the answer misses
→ 2–3 go-to tricks for getting a better answer, every time.
🛡️Safe & Smart AI Use

Quick hits: what's okay to share, and how to fact-check.

  • What's safe to type into an AI chat (and what never is)
  • How to spot something that looks true but isn't
  • When to pause and ask a trusted adult
⚠️ Full ground rules in our free AI Safety Chat.
💡Research & Learning

The topic that makes them groan? Let's fix that.

  • Pick the one topic that's a real struggle: fractions, French verbs, the water cycle, anything
  • Turn it into whatever clicks: a song, a short video, a comic, a one-pager
  • Test it: can they explain it back in their own words?
→ A study aid for their toughest topic, in the format that actually works for them.

Session 2: Create & Share (about 3 hrs)

✍️Creating With AI

Their idea. AI just helps build it out.

  • Start with their own story or script idea
  • Use AI to expand it, revise it, fill in the gaps
  • Finish with something whole, not just a fragment
→ A short story or script they shaped start to finish.
🎤Finding Your Voice

Write it, say it, fix it, say it better.

  • Write a short speech or pitch script with AI's help
  • Practice it out loud, with AI as the listener
  • Get feedback on pacing and wording, then run it again
  • Repeat until it actually feels ready
→ A real speech script, and the practice reps to deliver it with confidence.
🛠️Build & Play

That tough topic from earlier? Now it's a game.

  • Take that same tricky topic from Research & Learning (yes, even math questions)
  • Turn it into a real game: quiz battles, timed challenges, matching rounds
  • Add points, levels, or streaks, the dopamine hit that makes practice addictive (the good kind)
  • Play it, tweak it, play again, no coding required
→ A playable game that turns their hardest subject into their favorite way to practice.
